How Partner Statements are Produced Vehicle Partners and Owner Plans 1. Business Context and Purpose The Vehicle Owner Program is how the fleet grows without the club buying every car. An outside owner places their vehicle in the club’s hands and is compensated from what actually happens to it. Two records carry that. The partner is who the club pays. The plan is the deal for one car. A partner with three cars has one partner record and three plans, and every payout, statement and notification traces back through both. The rule that shapes everything else: a plan is a dated agreement, never an editable one. Terms change by ending one plan and starting the next, so a payout calculated under last year’s terms stays calculated under them. The partner is a payee, so these are the details a payment and a tax document need. A partner may also be a member, and the two records stay separate. Their membership does not change how they are paid, and their partnership does not change how they are billed. Deactivate, never delete. Payout history is money that changed hands. 2) Partner Contacts A partner may have several contacts, each with a role, a telephone number and an email address. Exactly one is primary at a time. The primary is who statements and payout notices go to, so the club always knows who to reach without anybody choosing in the moment. Contacts carry an active flag rather than effective dates. A person who leaves the partner’s business is deactivated; there is no need to reconstruct who held the role in March. Promoting a new primary demotes the old one in the same write. Two primaries is not a state that should be reachable. 3) Creating a Plan A plan covers one car. Set the vehicle, the partner, the four money levers, the effective dates and the status. The minimum is optional. Many plans carry none, and an absent minimum is not a minimum of zero - it means the floor logic does not run for that plan at all. Set the effective dates deliberately. They decide which plan a day or a trip is computed under, and the boundary matters more than it looks. Status runs Pending, Active, Suspended, Ended. Suspending pauses the arrangement without ending it and the car stays with the club; ending closes it. 4) Changing Terms Date and replace, never edit. End the current plan and start a new one with the new terms from the following day. Every ledger entry records the plan it was computed under. That is what makes a historical payout explainable after the terms have moved on. At a boundary, each day and each trip earns under the plan in effect when it happened. A day belongs to the plan covering that date; a trip belongs to the plan covering its end date, which is the same rule the period uses. Never delete a plan. A plan entered in error is ended on its start date, which leaves it visible and earning nothing. 5) Guarantee Groups A guarantee group is a named set of plans belonging to one partner whose minimums are settled together rather than car by car. Each plan keeps its own minimum. The group changes how they are tested, not what they are. A plan not in a group is a group of one. Build the calculation that way and there is no second code path: the ungrouped case falls out of the grouped one. Membership is dated, so a car joining or leaving is recorded rather than edited in place. The base fee needs no join handling - the daily entries prorate it by construction. A plan belongs to at most one group at a time. The calculation itself is in the payout periods guide. 4. Rules and Constraints A partner is the payee; a plan is the deal for one car. A partner may hold several plans; a plan covers exactly one vehicle. A partner may also be a member, and the records stay separate. Exactly one partner contact is primary at a time. Contacts carry an active flag rather than effective dates. The minimum is optional; absent is not zero. Terms change by dating and replacing, never by editing. Every ledger entry records the plan it was computed under. A day earns under the plan covering that date; a trip under the plan covering its end date. Suspending pauses the arrangement; ending closes it. Neither a partner nor a plan is ever deleted. A guarantee group belongs to one partner and holds plans that keep their own minimums. A plan belongs to at most one guarantee group at a time. An ungrouped plan is treated as a group of one. 5. Integration and Edge Cases A partner who is also a member.  Two records, two relationships. Their own trips in their own car earn nothing, which is covered in the vehicle use guide. A partner with three cars.  One partner record, three plans, and possibly one guarantee group across them. A plan with no minimum.  Ordinary. The floor logic does not run and the partner receives what the car earned. A plan ending mid-month.  Days up to the end date earn under it; days after earn under whatever follows, or nothing. A trip that starts under one plan and ends under the next.  It belongs to the plan covering its end date, matching the period rule. A car sold out of the program.  The plan ends. The ledger keeps every entry it ever produced. A partner contact who leaves.  Deactivated, and if they were primary another must be promoted in the same action. A car moving between guarantee groups.  The old membership is dated closed and a new one opened. It settles with whichever group held it for the days in question. 6. Automations and Notifications Each automation below is listed with its trigger and its expected outcome. A car joining mid-month brings only the days it was in the program. Availability Days 1. Business Context and Purpose The base fee is earned one day at a time. It is never paid as a lump sum and never prorated by a formula. A nightly evaluation asks one question of every active program vehicle - did the club have this car today - and writes one ledger entry for each day the answer is yes. That is the whole design, and its elegance is that proration never happens. A car available twenty-five days of thirty-one has twenty-five entries, which is twenty-five thirty-firsts of the base fee by construction. Nobody calculates a fraction, and the partner can verify their base compensation day by day rather than being handed a number. The same day count also drives the minimum guarantee where a plan carries one, so the two can never disagree. Pairs with How It Works chapter 16, section HOW VEHICLE USE AFFECTS THE PAYOUT. 2. A vehicle with no active plan is not evaluated at all. Ask one question: was this car in the club’s operational control and eligible for member reservations today. The status at end of day decides. A car that came back at four in the afternoon was available that day; a car that went out to its owner at four was not. One reading per day, taken at the end of it. A qualifying partial day counts as a full one. There are no half entries and no hours. The end-of-day reading is the only granularity, which is what keeps the arithmetic verifiable. 2) What Counts and What Does Not THE DAY TEST The car was... Does the day count? Ready and unbooked Yes. A car standing ready is exactly what the base fee pays for. Out with a member Yes. The club had it and put it to work. Held by an internal block, an event or marketing staging Yes. It stayed in the club’s hands. Under a club withhold Yes. The club chose not to release it, which is the club’s decision to make with a car it holds. With its own partner No. It was not in the club’s hands. At a vendor for service Depends. See the determination below. Outside the plan’s effective dates No. There is no plan to earn under. The principle behind the table: the base fee compensates the partner for the club having the car, not for the club using it. Anything that keeps the car in the club’s hands earns; anything that takes it out does not. A car nobody booked still earns, and that is deliberate rather than an oversight. Demand risk sits with the club. 3) The One Case That Waits A day the car is at a vendor is not written until the determination exists. The trip’s pay_vop_use answers whether the partner keeps earning through the work, and until it is answered nothing is posted for those days. Write nothing provisional. Do not post the days and reverse them, and do not post them as zero. There is nothing to correct because there was never anything wrong. When the answer arrives, write the whole span at once, dated to the days they belong to rather than the day the answer came. Earning writes an entry for every day of the span. Not Earning writes none. The determination and the task that produces it are specified in the vehicle use guide. 4) Writing the Entry One entry per qualifying day, of type VOP day, dated to that day. Value it at the daily rate: the plan’s base fee divided by the number of days in that period. February and March give different daily rates from the same base fee, which is correct. Record the plan the entry was computed under, so a payout stays explainable after the terms change. The job is idempotent. Running it twice for the same vehicle and day writes one entry. A missed night is caught up by the next run, because the job compares what should exist against what does. 5) What This Gives the Calculation Availability earnings are simply the sum of the period’s VOP day entries. No proration, no fraction, no formula. The minimum guarantee prorates on the same count. A car available seventeen of thirty-one days carries seventeen thirty-firsts of its floor. Deriving both from one count is what makes joining and leaving mid-period need no special handling. The summary shows both the plan’s full base fee and the availability-adjusted figure actually earned, so a partner can see what the days cost them. 4. Rules and Constraints The evaluation runs nightly for every vehicle with an active plan, and for no others. The status at end of day decides; a qualifying partial day counts as a full day. A day in the club’s hands counts, including internal blocks, events, marketing and club withholds. A day with the partner does not count. A day outside the plan’s effective dates does not count. A day at a vendor is not written until the trip’s determination exists. No provisional or zero-valued day entry is ever written. One entry per qualifying day, valued at the base fee divided by the days in that period. The job is idempotent and catches up after a missed run. Availability earnings are the sum of the day entries; nothing is prorated by formula. The minimum guarantee prorates on the same day count as the base fee. 5. Integration and Edge Cases A car that came back at four in the afternoon.  The day counts. End of day is the reading. A car collected by its owner at four in the afternoon.  The day does not count, by the same rule read the other way. A car under a club withhold for a fortnight.  Every day counts. The club held the car and chose not to release it, which is a decision about the club’s own inventory. A car at a vendor for eleven days spanning month end.  Nothing is written for any of those days until the determination is answered, then all eleven are written to the days they belong to. The March days land in March. A plan that starts on the twentieth.  Days from the twentieth earn; the nineteenth and earlier do not exist for this plan. A twenty-eight day February.  The daily rate is the base fee divided by twenty-eight, so a fully available February pays the full base fee, exactly as a fully available March does. The job missing a night.  The next run writes the missing day. Nothing is lost and nobody intervenes. A vehicle whose plan ended yesterday.  Not evaluated. There is nothing to earn under. A car available every day of the month.  Thirty-one entries, summing to exactly the base fee. 6. Developer Notes and Considerations One entry per day is the design, not an implementation detail. It is what removes proration entirely and what lets a partner check the arithmetic. Any optimization that stores a count instead has thrown away the auditability the whole model exists for. End of day, once.  Reading a status several times a day, or computing hours, produces a number nobody can verify and a partial day nobody can explain. Nothing provisional for vendor days.  Writing days and reversing them creates corrections in a ledger whose whole value is that it does not need them. The daily rate uses the days in that period.  A hard-coded thirty, or a year divided by twelve, quietly overpays some months and underpays others. Idempotency matters more here than almost anywhere, because the job runs unattended every night for every car. Compare against what exists rather than trusting that last night worked. A missing day is invisible.  It does not fail, it does not alert, and it silently reduces a payout. Monitor that the evaluation ran, not only that it succeeded. 8. Availability earnings equal the sum of the day entries with no formula applied. The guarantee prorates on the same day count. How Each Kind of Use Is Treated 1. Business Context and Purpose Every mile and every day of a program car’s life is one of four kinds of use: a member has it, the club is working with it (fuel runs, washing, lot moves, staging), the partner has it, or it has gone for service. Each raises the same questions - does the day count toward availability, do the miles earn, and who pays what the club spends. One rule answers the earning questions: the partner is compensated for the club’s use of the car, and not for use that is his own or attached to something he is not being paid for. Two of the three are decided by the platform. The third is decided by a person, because whether a partner should keep earning while their car is off the road depends on why it went, and no rule can read that from the data. That decision is recorded in one place: pay_vop_use on the trip. One answer, three consequences. Pairs with How It Works chapter 16, section HOW VEHICLE USE AFFECTS THE PAYOUT. 2. Implementation Workflow 1) Setting the Answer at Trip Creation Set it once, when the trip is created, from the program state at that moment. Never recompute it. A car joining the program in June must not acquire determinations for May. First test, is the vehicle in the program. If not, leave it null. No task, no question, no matter the trip type. Second test, only for program vehicles. A member trip is set to earning - unless the member is the vehicle’s own partner, in which case not earning. Either way no task is raised, because the answer is knowable. Every other trip type, present or future, is set to undetermined and raises the task. Name only the exceptions and default everything else, so a trip type added later inherits the safe answer rather than slipping through unnoticed. Undetermined and null are deliberately different. One means nobody has decided; the other means the question does not apply. A single nullable flag cannot express both, and the payout blocker depends on telling them apart without consulting current program membership. 2) The Determination Task Raise it when the trip is created, not when the car comes back. Whoever booked the work usually knows what it is for straight away, and waiting for the return wastes the window. The question put to the reviewer: does the vehicle partner continue to earn while this vehicle is out for service? Present both answers with all three consequences named, so nobody has to remember what they are. Costs have their own answer, separate from earning. Service cost attribution is three-way: partner-borne work posts as charges against the payout, each referencing the service record; member-borne work (damage from a member’s trip) bills to the member through the ordinary charge machinery and never touches the partner’s ledger; club-borne work stays with the club and appears on no one’s statement. There is no default on the form. The reviewer chooses. An unreviewed determination must never become a decision by inaction. Due in ten days. That is ample for a decision this simple and leaves the escalation room to work well before the money moves. The task is not shown on the service booking form and asks nothing of the branch booking the work. The club does not advertise which cars are in the program, and the branch does not need to know. Assign it to the position responsible for the program rather than a named individual. The responsibility layer is specified in the Automations chapter. 3) When a Member Uses the Car The day counts. The car was in the club’s hands and available, which is what the base fee pays for. The miles earn. Write a mileage entry valued at the plan’s per_mile_rate times the trip’s chargeable miles - the same showroom-to-showroom figure the member was charged for, including the staff delivery and collection legs and less any goodwill adjustment. Operational miles earn too. Club driving outside any member trip - a fuel run, a wash, a lot move, a staging drive, a branch transfer - is recorded on its own operational trip, and its miles earn for the partner at the same per_mile_rate: it is still the club using his car. The one exclusion: miles attached to a not-earning service trip, including its delivery legs to and from the vendor, earn nothing - they belong to a use the partner is not being paid for. One measurement, two ledgers. The member pays for those miles and the partner is paid for them, from the same number. Never derive the partner’s figure separately - recomputing from the odometer would miss the goodwill adjustment, and the two would disagree with nothing to reveal it. Incidental charges are the member’S. Fuel, tolls, excess mileage and damage are billed to the member in the ordinary way. None of it reaches the partner’s ledger. 4) When the Partner Uses the Car The day does not count. The car was with its owner rather than in the club’s hands. The miles do not earn. The partner’s compensation measures the club’s use of the car, never their own. The partner is charged what a member would have paid. Fuel if they bring the car back short, at cost plus the same markup a member pays. Tolls incurred while they had it, matched to the trip the same way. Anything else the club settles on their behalf. Each posts as a charge entry on the payout ledger, referencing the source record so every deduction traces to what caused it. The only difference from a member is where it lands. A member receives a bill; a partner has it deducted from what they are owed. Same rates, same records, different destination. Detecting this case means comparing the trip’s member to the vehicle’s partner. Where a partner drives their own car it is recorded as a member trip, so the comparison is the only thing that distinguishes it. 5) When the Car Goes for Service All three consequences follow pay_vop_use, and none of them is decided by the service reason, the vendor, or the plan. Earning: the days count, any miles driven earn at the per-mile rate, and any guarantee is not reduced. Not earning: the days do not count, the miles do not earn, and any guarantee prorates down with the days. Undetermined: nothing is written at all. No day entries, no mileage entry. See the availability guide. Costs are separate from earning. Where the agreement puts the work on the partner’s side, the cost posts as a charge referencing the service record, whichever way the earning question was answered. A partner can keep earning through work they are also paying for. A cost may be unknown when the trip closes. The invoice arrives later; the charge posts when it does, into whichever period is open then. Do not hold the trip open waiting for it. 4. Rules and Constraints pay_vop_use lives on the trip, is set once at creation, and is never recomputed. Null means the vehicle was not in the program; Undetermined means nobody has decided. A member trip is set automatically, to Not Earning where the member is the vehicle’s partner. Every other trip type defaults to Undetermined and raises the task. The determination task has no default answer and is due in ten days. The determination is never asked on the service booking form. A mileage entry is valued from the trip’s chargeable miles, never a separately derived figure. Member-borne costs never reach the partner’s ledger. Partner-borne incidental charges post at the same rates a member would pay. A partner using their own car is detected by comparing the trip’s member to the vehicle’s partner. Costs and earning are decided separately; a partner may earn through work they are paying for. A trip is never held open waiting for an invoice. 5. Write a VOP amount entry in the opposite direction with a description of what it corrects. It lands in the period open when it is made, not the period it corrects. Closed months are not reopened and no statement is ever reissued. An adjustment without a description is useless six months later. Require one. 4) The Prepaid Balance The balance is derived, never stored: the sum of top-up entries less recoupment entries. There is no balance column anywhere - not on the entry, not on the plan, not on the group. A statement showing a running balance computes it as it renders, line by line, exactly as the member point statement does. The figure is presentation, not storage. It moves at most twice a period - one top-up or one recoupment - so summing it costs nothing. This is the same choice the points ledger and the perk ledger both made, and there is no reason for this ledger to differ. It belongs to the guarantee group rather than to a single plan, because the advance that created it was calculated for the group. An ungrouped plan is a group of one, so the rule is the same. It never goes negative. Recoupment can only take what is owed; anything beyond that is simply paid. 5) Reading the Ledger A period’s figures are always a sum of entries. Nothing is stored as a total that could drift from what produced it. A partner’s statement is reconcilable to this ledger entry by entry. That is the test of whether the ledger is doing its job. Reporting reads the ledger. There is no separate statistics store, and adding one would create a second answer that eventually disagrees. 4. Business Context and Purpose A period is an accounting month. Month end fixes membership instantly and completely: every trip belongs to the period its end date falls in, and nothing that happens later changes it. A trip is payout-ready when its settlement is complete and, where a service determination was needed, the determination is answered - use this term everywhere. There is no separate closing process to build. The month ends automatically at month end; trips become payout-ready as they settle and their determinations land; and running the payouts is the action that finishes the month. Do not encode a day count for when the run happens - the ordering is the invariant. There is no reopening. A closed period is never recalculated and no statement is ever reissued. Anything found afterwards posts as a new entry in the currently open period, exactly as a bank handles a correction. Pairs with How It Works chapter 16, section HOW THE MONTHLY PAYOUT IS CALCULATED. 2. Implementation Workflow 1) Which Period an Entry Belongs To A trip belongs to the period its trip ended in. A trip that ended on 28 March belongs to March even if it settles in April. This is the same rule that decides which statement a member’s trip appears on. A day belongs to the period containing that day. A service span crossing month end writes its March days into March and its April days into April. An entry written after its period has been paid lands in the open period, keeping its own entry date. The entry date and the period are separate facts and neither is derived from the other. Create periods ahead of time so there is always an open one to write into. An entry with nowhere to go is a defect that surfaces at the worst moment. 2) Closing the Period Month end is automatic and fixes what belongs to the period - membership by trip end date. There is no separate close step: the payout run itself finishes a month, and its payout-ready test is the guard. The test waits on nothing else: not invoices, not paperwork - every trip payout-ready is the whole list. Closing marks the period closed and stops new entries being dated into it. It does not calculate anything and it does not pay anybody. From the moment it closes, surface what would block the run. Every undetermined service trip covering days in the period, listed by partner. The person who will run payouts should see this with weeks in hand rather than being stopped on the morning. 3) The Payout Run The run is the finishing step: it verifies every trip covering days in the period is payout-ready, refuses per partner (or per whole guarantee group) where one is not, and marks the paid entries processed. Refuse to produce a payout for a partner while any service trip covering days in the period is still Undetermined. Covering days, not merely ending in them - those days are exactly what the base fee and the guarantee are calculated from, so a trip that began on 20 March and is still running blocks March. The block is scoped to the partner, and therefore to their guarantee group, because a group cannot be settled with one car’s earnings unknown. Every other partner is paid normally. One forgotten task must never hold up everybody’s money. A refusal at the run is a last resort. If the ten-day task and its escalation have worked, it should never happen. 4) The Calculation Waterfall Sum the availability earnings - the period’s VOP day entries. This is vop_payout_summary.base_fee_adjusted. Sum the mileage earnings. Subtract the owner plan fee. Subtract service costs charged to the partner. Subtract fuel and toll charges. The result is the gross payout. Straight arithmetic on the ledger, with nothing derived. 5) The Minimum Guarantee Not every plan has one. Where minimum_amount_per_period is absent the floor logic does not run at all, and the partner receives the gross. The floor is flat in the ordinary course - quiet months never shrink it; low member demand simply produces a larger shortfall, advanced and recovered through the prepaid balance. Two exceptions, both partner-side: (1) the ten-day rule - when the vehicle was unavailable for partner reasons for more than ten days in a period (non-earning days are partner-caused by definition), that period’s guarantee is applied pro rata on the days the club had the car; ten or fewer such days leave it untouched. (2) partial periods - a plan starting or ending mid-period carries a pro rata guarantee for the partial period. Compare gross against the prorated floor. If gross is lower, the partner receives the floor and the difference posts as a top-up entry, increasing the group’s prepaid balance. 6) Recoupment Recovery comes only from the surplus above the floor. Compute the excess of gross over the prorated floor, and recoup the lesser of that excess and the outstanding balance. The partner never receives less than the floor in any period. Recoupment can only slow how fast a good month rises above it. It can never pull a payment below the floor, and a period is never paid at zero because of an advance. Write the recoupment as its own entry so the balance movement is visible rather than netted away. Where a partnership ends with a balance outstanding, do nothing clever. The final period settles by the same arithmetic, the balance stays visible on the ledger, and whatever remains is resolved outside the platform. 7) Guarantee Groups Calculate each plan’s gross and each plan’s prorated floor first. Every car reports what it earned and whether it met its own floor, whatever the group does next. Then test once at group level: pay the greater of the sum of the gross figures and the sum of the prorated floors. An ungrouped plan is a group of one, so there is one code path rather than two. Worked example. Three cars each with a six thousand floor, earning nine, five and four thousand. Separately the club pays 9,000 + 6,000 + 6,000 = 21,000. Grouped, the group earned 18,000 against a combined floor of 18,000, so the club pays 18,000. The partner receives every dollar their cars earned and the strong car covers the quiet ones. Allocate any top-up back across the cars that were short, in proportion to how far each fell below its own floor, so every car’s ledger shows what it received. Nothing is hidden by grouping. A car in the group for only part of the period brings only the days it was there, because both the base fee and the floor already prorate on available days. 8) Writing the Summary Write one VOP_PAYOUT_SUMMARY per plan per period. vop_payout_summary.base_fee_original is the plan’s full base fee and vop_payout_summary.base_fee_adjusted is what the day entries came to. Keeping both is what lets a partner see exactly what the unavailable days cost them. Neither of them is the guarantee. Mark every entry in the period processed once the run completes. The summary is derived from the ledger and reconcilable to it entry by entry. If it is not, the ledger is right. 4. A trip belongs to the period its trip ended in; a day to the period containing that day. An entry written after its period was paid lands in the open period and keeps its own date. Periods are created ahead of time so there is always an open one. The payout run is a separate step, days after the close. The run refuses a partner while any service trip covering days in the period is Undetermined. The block is scoped to that partner and their guarantee group; every other partner is paid.
